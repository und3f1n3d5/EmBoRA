## Definitions and independent premise assessment

Implementation output O is a fourth object. For known-origin rows record R-I (input conversion), B-R (arithmetic), B-I (total numerical residual), and O-B (implementation discrepancy) separately. For unknown-origin rows actual I, R-I and B-I are unavailable and are omitted; retain R/B/B-R. No counterfactual I is published as actual diagnostics. Fractions are signed residuals; absolute residuals are obtained after subtraction.

The premise is confirmed without importing production projection, urgency or engine, and without reading production observed values as expected. ## Proposed operation graph and runtime assumptions

Decimal JSON/YAML numeric tokens mean their exact decimal rationals at the I layer. Convert once with IEEE-754 binary64 round-to-nearest, ties-to-even (RN); do not use the token's shortest reprinted decimal as the original ideal input. Reject nonfinite values, duplicate keys, booleans as numbers, string-number coercion and illegal domains before clipping. Executable assumption: 53-bit binary64 stored after every arithmetic node, round-to-nearest-even, gradual underflow, no FTZ/DAZ, no excess-precision intermediates, no fast-math, FMA contraction or reassociation, no compiler/vector reduction replacing declared order. ## Bounds and their use

Real I and R recurrence obey vhat in [0,1], |u|<=alpha, |V|<=alpha and L<=alpha. Strict monotonicity in q is a real statement; rounded q/appraisals can coalesce and identity handling can produce tiny local ordering differences for nearly equal arbitrary helper targets. Never assume universal strict binary64 monotonicity over every nextafter target.

Let eta=2^-52, e=epsilon_B>0. This conservative per-node absolute rounding envelope applies to numbers in [0,2], including gradual subnormals. In the nonidentity graph, compared with the exact recurrence on rounded inputs, coefficient error<=eta, the two product errors<=eta each, sum error<=eta; projection error<=4eta because clip is 1-Lipschitz. Delta subtraction adds <=eta, so Delta error<=5eta. Positive denominator error<=eta; negative denominator is an exact selected input. Both H and H_R are >=e. For the real model |Delta_R|/H_R<=alpha_B; hence denominator sensitivity contributes <=alpha_B*eta/e. Before urgency clipping the division operand ratio is <=1/e; its own rounding contributes <=eta/e plus a subnormal allowance. Crossing Delta=0 uses continuity of the signed two-branch function; each branch is Lipschitz 1/e, so the same bound covers crossed signs. Thus the **conservative diagnostic envelope** `|B-R|<=8*eta/e` covers all fixed-parameter legal scalar projections, with margin for subnormal rounding. Identity B=R exactly. Numerically this envelope is about 1.77636e-9; it is not an implementation tolerance and was not fit to the failed fixture.

For known ideal inputs define e_min=min(epsilon_I,epsilon_B)>0, a_max=max(alpha_I,alpha_B). One conservative input envelope, from piecewise derivative bounds and continuity at q=v, is:

`E_input = |da|/e_min + a_max*|dq|/e_min + a_max*|dv|*(1/e_min+1/e_min^2) + a_max*|de|/e_min^2`.

Use path bounds with all intermediate alpha/epsilon in the declared domain. This envelope is deliberately loose; exact Fraction residual decomposition is preferable for fixtures. |B-I|<=E_input+8eta/e. Endpoints, signed zero and clipped branches still use the continuous piecewise map. A scientific-to-executable residual beyond its independently derived bound fails numerical diagnostics; it cannot be excused by an O-B pass. Unknown ideal inputs are labelled unknown: actual ideal_inputs, I, R-I, B-I, input_bound and total_bound are absent. The input and total envelopes above are evaluated only with known original exact inputs; arithmetic B-R and its envelope remain available for known binary64 inputs. Optional counterfactuals require a separate explicitly labelled object and cannot populate actual fields; this release emits none.

Executable guards enforce finite [0,1] vhat and [-1,1] urgency exactly, semantic identity and endpoint impossible signs exactly, and the diagnostic tighter envelope `|u_B|<=alpha_B+8eta/e`. The old exact-alpha upper bound is not a hard binary64 assertion: rounded recurrence can slightly exceed alpha (e.g. v=0.5,q=1). F-09's exact-alpha bound remains real; ordered normalized weights and accumulation add their own numerical residuals, so validators compare to their executable graph rather than clip V/L to alpha. The generic production urgency saturation at 1 remains unreachable because alpha_B+8eta/e<1; wider-helper urgency saturation is retained separately. No bound creates a branch epsilon.

## Reachability and finite saturation

Real fixed-alpha recurrence from strictly interior neutral v=.5 never reaches 0 or 1 at finite time. Binary64 cannot inherit that assertion just from a real proof. Here fixed alpha_B is about .35 and c_B about .65. Literal projection is monotone in each input before the identity node; the exact witness F05(nextafter(1,0),1)=nextafter(1,0) is the maximal nonidentity interior output, and F05(min_subnormal,0)=min_subnormal the minimal one. Identity remains interior. Consequently this **particular** rounded recurrence also cannot newly hit an endpoint from an interior binary64 state. It can stagnate at representable interior values, unlike the strict real recurrence.

Binary64 input conversion can map an ideal positive value <half min-subnormal to zero, or an ideal value close enough to one to one; those are finite representation saturation cases, not neutral trajectory reachability. General helpers with other update coefficients/evaluation policies can hit endpoints in finite arithmetic, but alpha is fixed here and such overrides are not legal model configurations. Urgency=1 is a legal wider-helper saturation case with prev=desired=.5,current=.6, not a production recurrence. Nothing here establishes neutral-reachable prevalence of near-epsilon states or frequency of stagnant states in the 200-round frame. No trajectory was run.
